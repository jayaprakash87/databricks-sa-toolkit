# Customer Discovery

## Goal
Understand the problem before proposing architecture or products.

## Workflow
1. Clarify the business objective and decision/workflow to improve.
2. Identify sponsors, users, operators, technical owners, security, and finance stakeholders.
3. Understand the current-state process and platform landscape.
4. Identify pain points and their business consequences.
5. Define measurable success criteria.
6. Clarify functional requirements.
7. Clarify NFRs:
   - scale and growth
   - freshness/latency
   - concurrency
   - availability/SLA
   - recovery
   - security
   - privacy/residency
   - cost constraints
8. Clarify sources, grains, keys, change semantics, quality issues, and ownership.
9. State assumptions and unresolved risks.
10. Ask only questions that can change value, scope, architecture, or delivery.

## Discovery validation
When prior context exists, validate rather than restarting from zero.

## Guardrails
- No feature-first selling.
- Do not ask low-value questions for completeness.
- Separate customer facts from assumptions.
- Do not disguise technical curiosity as discovery.

## Output
1. business objective
2. persona/stakeholder map
3. current state
4. pains and consequences
5. success metrics
6. functional requirements
7. NFRs
8. data/source context
9. assumptions and risks
10. prioritized discovery questions
