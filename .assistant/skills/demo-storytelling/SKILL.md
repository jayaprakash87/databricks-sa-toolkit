# Demo Storytelling

## Goal
Use the prototype as evidence of the future state, not as the meeting agenda.

## Demo pattern
Persona → current friction → action → future state → result → business implication.

## Workflow
1. Define the persona and business decision.
2. Define what the audience should believe after the demo.
3. Choose 3–6 demo scenes.
4. For every scene define:
   - user action
   - what changes
   - why it matters
   - relevant architecture capability
   - business implication
5. Decide when to show architecture.
6. Prepare a fallback for live failures.
7. Separate demo shortcuts from production design.
8. close by reconnecting to original customer outcomes.

## Avoid
Notebook → table → pipeline → endpoint → dashboard as the narrative.

## Guardrails
- Do not show functionality solely because it exists.
- Do not oversell mocked or precomputed behavior.
- Do not use technical jargon with nontechnical personas unless needed.
- Do not let a demo failure end the customer conversation.

## Output
1. demo hypothesis
2. persona
3. scene-by-scene story
4. business message per scene
5. architecture transition
6. fallback
7. production differences
8. close
